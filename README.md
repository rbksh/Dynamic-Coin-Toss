# Dynamic Coin Toss - Independent Research Project
**Author:** Shesh Shiromani

This is an independent research project investigating the deterministic physical dynamics of a coin toss and its intersection with human cognitive bias. Built entirely in Python, the project explores how Chaos Theory acts as the mathematical translator between the absolute determinism of physical laws and the statistical illusions categorized by the Law of Small Numbers.

## Core Research Focus

*   **Parametric Analysis:** Analyzing the specific physical parameters that govern a coin in flight, including initial upward velocity, angular momentum, moment of inertia, and aerodynamic friction. The core premise is that a coin toss is not a random 50/50 statistical event, but a highly sensitive mechanical system dictated entirely by its initial conditions.
*   **The Law of Small Numbers:** Examining the psychological phenomenon where human observers incorrectly assume that a small sequence of events will reflect the theoretical probability distribution over a large parent dataset. This covers the cognitive friction that occurs when natural clusters or streaks appear, which the human brain rejects as "non-random."
*   **Chaos Theory:** Demonstrating how deterministic physical systems with extreme sensitivity to initial conditions produce data that humans perceive as pure statistical randomness. Chaos theory acts as the boundary layer explaining why physical predictability breaks down into psychological and statistical uncertainty.

## System Architecture & Progression

The computational phase of this research utilizes machine learning and rigorous physical modeling to attempt pattern recognition within chaotic datasets. As the research has progressed, the architecture has been split into distinct simulation environments, viewable in the repository structure.

### 1. The DHM Base Model (`/coin_toss_dhm_research`)
This module replicates the core premise of the Diaconis-Holmes-Montgomery (DHM) physics model. 
*   **Mechanics:** It restricts the physical parameters to basic initial conditions (spin rate, upward velocity, angle) to prove that a coin toss inherently favors the side it started on.
*   **Machine Learning:** Utilizes a Physics-Informed Neural Network (`pinn_model.py`) working in tandem with `physics_engine.py` to ingest these parameters and deterministically predict the binary outcome (Heads or Tails). 
*   **Execution:** Orchestrated via `master_simulation.py`.

### 2. 5 Degrees of Freedom (DoF) Analysis (`/dhm-research-test-with-dof`)
This represents the most advanced phase of the research, heavily expanding the physical complexity of the simulation to account for a tumbling object in 3D space.
*   **Complex Dynamics:** Incorporates 5 Degrees of Freedom into the physical parameters, mapping precession, nutation, and spatial translation over time via an advanced `physics_engine.py`.
*   **Diagnostic Telemetry:** Employs a suite of metric trackers (`metrics_error.py`, `metrics_efficiency.py`, `metrics_progress.py`) to quantify the degradation of the predictive model (`model_predictor.py`) as physical complexity increases. 
*   **Covariance Tracking:** Generates live graphs (`diagnostic_covariance.py`) measuring the covariance between input perturbations and predictive breakdown, proving exactly how fast a physical system evades deterministic forecasting.
*   **Execution:** Handled via `run_all.py` to synchronize the physics engine, predictors, and `animated_visualizer.py`.

### 3. Root Chaotic Sequence Baseline
*   The original simulation framework utilizing TensorFlow and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural networks to analyze sequences generated by the logistic map equation. It serves as the mathematical baseline for tracking how rapidly a chaotic system evades prediction, visualized via `animated_simulation.py` in the root directory.
